A* Search

Finding the cheapest route across Riverford

How to read this

The problem. Drive across Riverford as cheaply as possible. A road's cost is its length times its congestion, so the shortest-looking route is often not the cheapest. A river splits the city and there are only two bridges.

The three numbers. For any intersection, g is what you have already paid to reach it, h is the straight-line distance still to go — a guess, drawn on the map as the dashed line — and f = g + h is the estimated cost of a whole trip through there. f is the only number the queue sorts on.

Why the guess is safe. Roads bend, and traffic only ever slows you down, so the crow-flies distance can never be more than the real remaining cost. A guess that never overestimates is called admissible, and it is the reason A* never misses the best route.

Watch for these. The search heads straight at the goal, reaches the water where there is no bridge, and has to back out. It also walks into Pier Road, a dead end aimed right at the destination. And the winning route starts by driving away from the goal, onto the fast belt road.

Compare with Dijkstra. The button in the header splits the map in two. The right-hand pane is not a different algorithm — it is this same search with the heuristic switched off, so it sorts on g alone. Both panes always show the same number of intersections settled, so you are watching how each one spent the same amount of work. Dijkstra spreads outward in all directions; A* stretches toward the goal.
